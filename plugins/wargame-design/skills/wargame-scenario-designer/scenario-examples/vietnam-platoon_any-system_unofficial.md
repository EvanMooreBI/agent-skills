# Vietnam platoon: card-driven skirmish scenario format

**Rules system:** Any platoon-level, card-driven system, such as Too Fat Lardies-style games
**Scale:** Platoon and company
**Presentation:** Separate briefings for each side plus umpire's notes

This file describes a common layout in original words. It contains no scenario text from a published source. For maps, use the `wargame-scenario-map-making` skill.

## Document structure

Three pages:

1. Briefing for side A: timeline, objectives, forces, and deployment.
2. Briefing for side B: objectives, forces, deployment, and fortifications.
3. Umpire's notes: map, terrain rules, civilian placement, and the card deck listing.

## Briefing

Each side's briefing holds only what that side knows:

- **Timeline:** one sentence of strategic context.
- **Briefing:** one paragraph in second person, with primary and secondary objectives and their victory points.
- **Forces:** by platoon, with leader levels, squad sizes, weapon types, and quality ratings in the rules' terms.
- **Deployment:** which table edges and which method, such as hidden markers.

## OOB format

Group by formation, giving each role, its size, and its weapon type. List quality and weapon notes after the OOB. Any random extra leaders get a stated die roll.

## Asymmetric deployment

- The attacker enters on hidden markers from a chosen edge.
- The defender deploys hidden and builds defenses within stated limits on length, count, and spacing, such as trenches, tunnels, bunkers, and mines.

## Asymmetric victory conditions

Each side has a different primary and secondary objective with its own victory points. For example, one side evacuates civilians while the other sets ambushes.

## Umpire's notes

- The map, with terrain types and their effects.
- Where civilian groups are placed.
- The card deck, listed by side and general cards.

## Structural lessons

- Asymmetric objectives force different approaches instead of mirror-image play.
- Hidden deployment with defenses placed by the player creates decisions before the game.
- Umpire's notes hold what neither player should see.
- Card-driven activation needs the deck listed in full.
- Civilians as active game elements add moral weight.
