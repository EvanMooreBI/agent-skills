# WWII company: Flames of War standard mission format

**Rules system:** Flames of War. Other Battlefront systems share this template.
**Scale:** Company (teams on bases)
**Presentation:** The publisher's standard-mission template

When you design a standard, non-historical mission for a Battlefront system, follow this structure. This file describes the template in original words. It reproduces no mission text, tables, or rules values. Take the reserve timing, scoring values, and mission list from your own copy of the publisher's mission pack. For maps, use the `wargame-scenario-map-making` skill.

## Document structure

Each mission is a two-page spread:

- **Page 1:** a deployment diagram, then Setting Up, Deployment, Who Goes First, and Winning the Game.
- **Page 2:** the special rules the mission uses, such as ambushes, reserves, victory points, and holding objectives.

## Choosing a mission

The publisher offers three methods: a random roll, agreement between the players, and a matrix that pairs each player's chosen battle plan (attack, maneuver, or defend).

## Mission template

### Setting up

1. Decide attacker and defender.
2. Pick table edges or quarters.
3. Place objectives with exact distance constraints in both inches and centimeters.

### Deployment

1. Decide which units start in reserve.
2. Choose ambushes.
3. Place any pre-registered artillery markers.
4. Deploy units in their zones.
5. Apply any starting conditions, such as dug-in infantry.

### Who goes first

Usually the attacker, or a die roll in meeting engagements. Meeting engagements often limit the first player to offset the first-move advantage.

### Winning the game

The attacker usually wins by holding an objective from a set turn onward. The defender wins by preventing that. Some missions also end on a force-morale failure.

## Deployment diagram

- A different color for each side's deployment zone.
- The table centerline.
- Every measurement in both inches and centimeters.
- Objective placement areas.
- Reserve arrival points or edges.

## Reserves and scoring

The mission pack defines several reserve types. They differ in when units can arrive, which edge they use, and whether the edge is random. It also defines a victory-point scale that rewards a decisive win over a costly one. Name the reserve type and the scoring rule your mission uses. Don't copy their tables.

## Structural lessons

- Give every measurement in both inches and centimeters.
- The deployment diagram matters most, and its zone boundaries must be exact.
- Reserve rules create asymmetry without changing the forces.
- Objective placement rules prevent degenerate positions.
- Scoring should reward a decisive result, not a grinding one.
