# WWII company: Fireball Forward publisher format

**Rules system:** Fireball Forward
**Scale:** Company
**Presentation:** The publisher's compact two-page scenario layout

This file describes the publisher's layout in original words. It contains no scenario text, OOBs, or rules values. For a real scenario, use your own copy of the publisher's book. For maps, use the `wargame-scenario-map-making` skill.

## Document structure

Each scenario takes a two-page spread:

- **Left column:** the title, scenario rules, victory points, flank-attack options, game length, and aftermath.
- **Right side:** a deployment map with a north arrow, setup areas, and labeled terrain.

## Sections

1. **Title:** a short, evocative name.
2. **Date and place:** the date, time of day, and location.
3. **History:** one short paragraph.
4. **Scenario rules:** a bullet list covering terrain, weather, and ammunition limits.
5. **Victory points:** values that differ by unit type, plus points for buildings and key locations.
6. **Flank attack (optional):** a commitment made before the game: which units, which edge, and which turn.
7. **Game length:** a fixed number of turns.
8. **Aftermath:** one paragraph on the historical result.
9. **Deployment map:** table size, north arrow, setup lines, and labeled terrain.

## Force briefing

Each side gets its own briefing:

- the force title with its full historical designation;
- an OOB by platoon, with each element's quality rating in the rules' format;
- a small die-roll table of force variants;
- one sentence of victory conditions;
- the side's initiative allowance;
- setup: how many units, which edge, and on which turn.

## Deployment zones

Named setup lines on the map, with distances from the table edges, define the zones. Entry edges, including optional ones, are labeled. Any flank-attack entry gives its distance limits.

## Structural lessons

- A complete scenario fits in two pages.
- Victory points that vary by unit type reward sensible risk.
- A small force-variant table adds replay value.
- Commitments made before the game, such as a flank attack, add a layer of generalship.
- Weather and ammunition limits work well as scenario rules.
- The aftermath paragraph lets players compare their result with history.
