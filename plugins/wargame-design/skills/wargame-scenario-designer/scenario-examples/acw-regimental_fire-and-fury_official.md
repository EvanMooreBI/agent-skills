# ACW regimental: Regimental Fire and Fury publisher format

**Rules system:** Regimental Fire and Fury
**Scale:** Regimental. Each unit is an infantry regiment, a cavalry squadron, or an artillery battery or section.
**Presentation:** The publisher's scenario layout, with a hierarchical OOB of cut-out unit labels.

This file describes the layout of the publisher's scenarios in original words. It contains no scenario text or data. For a real scenario, use your own copy of the publisher's scenario book. For maps, use the `wargame-scenario-map-making` skill.

## Document structure

A scenario usually runs to several pages, in this order:

1. Title page with an overview, historical context, and a terrain photo.
2. Terrain rules, a stand-count summary, and a deployment map with every unit placed.
3. The Union order of battle as a label tree.
4. The Confederate order of battle as a label tree.
5. Victory conditions and special scenario rules.

## Overview

- Title with the date of the action.
- Two or three paragraphs of history: the strategic situation, what happened, and any counterfactual the scenario assumes. State the counterfactual plainly.
- Player count, playing time, table size, and figure scale.
- Terrain: refer to the base terrain rules and list only additions and changes.

## OOB as a label tree

The OOB is a visual hierarchy, Army to Corps to Division to Brigade to Regiment or Battery, joined by NATO-style symbols. Each unit is a black label with three lines: the parent brigade in parentheses, the unit designation, and then quality, strength, and weapon code. An illustrative label, not taken from any published scenario:

```
(Brigade commander surname)
12 XX
Vet 10/8/6 RM
```

- Quality codes are the rules' experience ratings.
- Strength values follow the rules' stand-loss format.
- Weapon codes are the rules' short abbreviations for infantry, cavalry, and artillery weapons.
- Leaders carry the rules' leader ratings. Exceptional regimental commanders get the rules' special designation.

### Footnotes

Lowercase letters on labels point to deployment notes, such as "starts limbered in reserve" or "enters on turn 3". Footnotes keep labels short while still covering complex starting conditions.

## Stand-count summary

A table at the top of the OOB lists stands by type for each side: infantry, infantry command, mounted and dismounted cavalry, artillery with limbers, and each leader tier, with totals. It tells players how many figures to bring. Calculate the counts from your own OOB.

## Deployment map

A full-page map shows named terrain, roads, elevation features, every unit label at its start position, lettered reference points used by the deployment and retreat rules, a scale bar for each supported figure scale, and a north arrow.

## Deployment rules

Number or letter the rules for each side. Say which units start on the table and where, which enter later and at which reference point, in what formation, and any special starting status. Asymmetric starting states, such as one side starting fresh and the other worn, add tension without changing force size.

## Victory conditions

The publisher's scenarios usually give several independent victory conditions, such as a casualty threshold for each side, a greater-losses condition, and holding a key terrain feature. The side that achieves more of them wins. Reaching a condition can also impose a penalty on the side that suffers it.

## Special rules

Keep them few, and tie each one to the history. Typical subjects are an opening artillery-only phase, units starting broken, cavalry restrictions, and ammunition shortages.

## Structural lessons

- A label tree with symbols makes command relationships visible at a glance.
- Weapon codes convey equipment without long descriptions.
- Footnotes handle complex deployment without cluttering labels.
- Asymmetric starting states create tension through status rather than force size.
- The stand-count table is the players' shopping list.
- Several independent victory conditions create layered decisions.
- Named terrain on the map makes deployment text precise.
