# WWII company: Flames of War historical scenario format

**Rules system:** Flames of War, and other Battlefront systems that share its format
**Scale:** Company (teams on bases)
**Presentation:** The publisher's historical-scenario layout

This file describes the publisher's layout in original words. It contains no scenario text, OOBs, or rules. For a real scenario, use your own copy of the publisher's book. For maps, use the `wargame-scenario-map-making` skill.

## Document structure

Usually three pages:

1. Historical narrative and per-side orders.
2. A full-page deployment map with labeled zones, terrain, objectives, and measurements.
3. An OOB for each side, organized by formation, with the rules' skill and motivation ratings.

## Narrative and orders

- A title and one or two paragraphs that set the strategic context.
- Orders for each side in italic second person ("Your company must...").

## Deployment map

- Deployment areas with exact dimensions in both inches and centimeters.
- Objective markers with placement constraints, such as a minimum distance from the centerline.
- Terrain labeled on the map.
- A different shading for each side's zone.

## OOB format

Two columns for each side. The left column gives the historical unit name. The right column gives the matching game unit from the rules. The formation header carries the formation's historical designation and its rating. Support and reserves are listed separately from the core formation. An illustrative layout, with invented content:

```
[HISTORICAL FORMATION]                [GAME FORMATION]
[DIVISION]                            ([RATING])

Company HQ                            [command team]
1st Platoon                           [game unit]
2nd Platoon                           [game unit]

SUPPORT
[parent battalion]                    [game unit]
```

## Setup and victory

- Numbered setup steps: terrain, objectives, each side's deployment, and independent teams.
- A turn limit, an objective-based win condition, the rules' force-morale ending, and any victory-point scoring the scenario adds.
- Name supplement special rules. Don't rewrite them.

## Structural lessons

- Pairing the historical designation with the game unit keeps both history and play clear.
- Show ratings prominently for each formation.
- Give every zone measurement in both inches and centimeters.
- Second-person orders build immersion.
- List reserves separately from starting forces.
- Victory points that combine turns held with objectives keep play dynamic.
